Child safety standards — Nook

Last updated: 25 November 2026

These are the standards of the Nook app (Google Play: com.nguyenanhtuan.nook, operated by Tuan Nguyen) against child sexual abuse and exploitation. This is the English translation of the Vietnamese standards; if the two ever differ, the Vietnamese version governs.

Zero tolerance

Nook absolutely prohibits all child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE), including:

  • Images, videos or any other material that sexually abuses children (CSAM), including drawn, edited or machine-generated material
  • Content that sexualises anyone under 18
  • Befriending or grooming a child for sexual purposes
  • Sextortion, and trafficking

Violations lead to removal of the content and a permanent ban, without warning.

Built to be hard to abuse

  • Users must be 13 or older.
  • There is no public feed, no searching for strangers and no followers. Photos and messages reach only people who are already friends, and a friendship needs the other person to accept.
  • Photos and videos have no public links.

Reporting

  • In the app: on someone's photo, tap “…” and choose Report this post, or Block so they can no longer reach you. On the Friends screen, tap “…” next to a name to block or unfriend.
  • By email: Developers9X@gmail.com, even if you do not have a Nook account.

If a child is in immediate danger, call your local emergency number; in Vietnam, the national child protection hotline 111 or the nearest police.

What we do

  • Every report about child safety is reviewed first, as soon as it arrives.
  • Violating content is removed and the accounts involved are permanently banned.
  • Child sexual abuse material is preserved as evidence and reported to the authorities: the Department of Cyber Security and High-Tech Crime Prevention (Ministry of Public Security) in Vietnam, and NCMEC (CyberTipline) for international cases.
  • We cooperate with law enforcement on lawful requests.

Nook complies with child protection law, including Vietnam's Law on Children 2016.
